Further reading

The books the places and their stories come from, and those that let you go further.

  • Tobias Churton, Aleister Crowley in Paris: Sex, Art, and Magick in the City of Light, Inner Traditions, 2022.

    The main source for this map: Crowley’s Paris years, address by address, with the artists, the lovers and the magical operations.

  • Tobias Churton, Occult Paris: The Lost Magic of the Belle Époque, Inner Traditions, 2016.

    The book behind the thirty places of occult Paris: the esoteric bookshops, salons, magi and musicians of the Belle Époque, from Bailly to Satie.

  • Richard Kaczynski, Perdurabo: The Life of Aleister Crowley, North Atlantic Books, revised edition, 2010.

    The standard biography, thoroughly documented, with a precise chronology that makes it possible to date most of the stays.

  • Lawrence Sutin, Do What Thou Wilt: A Life of Aleister Crowley, St. Martin's Press, 2000.

    A balanced and readable biography, as attentive to the literary work as to the magic.

  • Aleister Crowley, The Confessions of Aleister Crowley, ed. John Symonds and Kenneth Grant, 1969.

    His “autohagiography”: lively and invaluable, but to be read with some distance, as Crowley often arranges the facts in his own favour.

  • Ellic Howe, The Magicians of the Golden Dawn, Routledge & Kegan Paul, 1972.

    The history of the Golden Dawn based on its archives, including the 1900 crisis and Mathers’s role in Paris.

  • Mary K. Greer, Women of the Golden Dawn, Park Street Press, 1995.

    Four women of the Order, including Moina Mathers: the best way into the Matherses’ Paris and the Rites of Isis.

  • Ithell Colquhoun, Sword of Wisdom: MacGregor Mathers and the Golden Dawn, Neville Spearman, 1975.

    A biography of Mathers by a Surrealist painter and occultist, rich in first-hand accounts.

  • Jacques Hillairet, Dictionnaire historique des rues de Paris, Éditions de Minuit, 1963.

    For the history of every street and every building: the essential tool for finding old addresses.
