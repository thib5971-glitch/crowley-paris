  1. 3
    The Mathers in Montmartre28, rue Saint-Vincent, 75018 Paris · Metro Lamarck-Caulaincourt (12)Private, seen from the streetLodging1901–1902

    Mathers wrote to Yeats from this address on 12 January 1901. At the end of 1902, having fallen out with him, Crowley came here to claim his books and luggage: Mathers returned the books but claimed he had to look for the luggage, which he had no doubt sold. The street, in the heart of old Montmartre, has hardly changed.

  2. 13
    Grande Loge de France (1903–1911)42, rue Rochechouart, 75009 Paris · Metro Anvers (2)Private, seen from the streetInitiation1904

    The headquarters and temples of the Grande Loge de France from 1903 to 1911. Crowley was received here into the Anglo-Saxon Lodge no. 343: initiated on 8 October 1904, raised Master on 17 December, registered as a “poet”. In June 1913 he resigned, reproaching the Grande Loge de France for tolerating Co-Masonry.

  3. 20
    Hotel on rue Frochot12, rue Frochot, 75009 Paris · Metro Pigalle (2, 12)Fate unknownLodging1926–1927

    His Pigalle address, at the foot of Montmartre, from 26 August 1926: the Hôtel Atlantic, called the Hôtel Frochot in his letters of 1927. The actress Jane Wolfe, a veteran of the Abbey of Thelema who became his secretary in Tunisia, joined him here on 12 September. This was the period of the magical operations recorded in his diary. He was still here in July 1927.
