Maria de Naglowska in Montparnasse

The “priestess of love” of the 1930s: her hotel, her cafés, her hall on rue Vavin and the church where she prayed.

On foot · about 30 min

  1. 131
    Maria de Naglowska's first hotelRue Bréa, 75006 Paris · Metro Vavin (4)Fate unknownResidencec. 1930-1931

    Arriving in Paris around 1930 with the promise of a job in publishing, Maria de Naglowska was refused a work permit. Short of money, she took a hotel room on rue Bréa, in the heart of Montparnasse: this is where her Parisian life began.

  2. 132
    La Rotonde105, boulevard du Montparnasse, 75006 Paris · Metro Vavin (4)Still openSalon1930-1931

    In her early days in Paris, Maria de Naglowska made the Rotonde her office and her salon: she received visitors, wrote and gave her first talks there, and the café owners sometimes treated her to a meal.

  3. 133
    La Coupole102, boulevard du Montparnasse, 75014 Paris · Metro Vavin (4)Still openSalon1931-1936

    Maria de Naglowska then moved her headquarters to the Coupole, which had opened in 1927. Almost every evening around 7 p.m. she held court in the “occultists' corner”, near the winter garden, where disciples, the curious and local artists crowded in.

  4. 134
    The lecture hall on rue Vavin36, rue Vavin, 75006 Paris · Metro Vavin (4) / Notre-Dame-des-Champs (12)Fate unknownInitiatic order1932-1936

    Every Wednesday, Maria de Naglowska rented a hall on rue Vavin for her lectures on the “Third Era” and the mystery of sex. Thirty to forty people squeezed in; it was also the home of the Brotherhood of the Golden Arrow. She gave her last lecture there in early 1936, before leaving Paris.

    Church of Notre-Dame-des-Champs91, boulevard du Montparnasse, 75006 Paris · Metro Notre-Dame-des-Champs (12)Still openPlace of worship1934-1935

    According to her disciple Marc Pluquet, the priestess of the Golden Arrow came every afternoon to pray in this church on boulevard du Montparnasse, a stone's throw from her hall on rue Vavin.
