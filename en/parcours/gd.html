  1. 2
    At the Mathers’, Ahathoor Temple87, avenue Mozart, 75016 Paris · Metro Jasmin (9)Private, seen from the streetInitiation1900

    The Matherses' villa. It has three entrances (avenue Mozart, rue Ribéra and rue de la Source), hence the impression of several addresses, and housed the Ahathoor Temple no. 7, with the Vault of the Adepts painted by Moina. Rejected by the London temple, Crowley, who arrived in Paris on 16 January 1900, was initiated Adeptus Minor here by Mathers after about a week, under the motto Parzival. In April he returned and became their envoy against the London revolt.

  2. 1
    Théâtre de la Bodinière18, rue Saint-Lazare, 75009 Paris · Metro Saint-Lazare (3, 12, 13, 14)GoneBackground1899

    The Matherses gave their “Rites of Isis” here in semi-public sessions, announced as “lectures” in January 1900. Crowley would write to Gerald Kelly that he had seen them officiate here; at one of these sessions he fell for the singer Lucile Hill, Susan Strong's understudy. The setting of the Parisian Golden Dawn, which he joined shortly afterwards. The hall has disappeared.

  3. 13
    Grande Loge de France (1903–1911)42, rue Rochechouart, 75009 Paris · Metro Anvers (2)Private, seen from the streetInitiation1904

    The headquarters and temples of the Grande Loge de France from 1903 to 1911. Crowley was received here into the Anglo-Saxon Lodge no. 343: initiated on 8 October 1904, raised Master on 17 December, registered as a “poet”. In June 1913 he resigned, reproaching the Grande Loge de France for tolerating Co-Masonry.

  4. 3
    The Mathers in Montmartre28, rue Saint-Vincent, 75018 Paris · Metro Lamarck-Caulaincourt (12)Private, seen from the streetLodging1901–1902

    Mathers wrote to Yeats from this address on 12 January 1901. At the end of 1902, having fallen out with him, Crowley came here to claim his books and luggage: Mathers returned the books but claimed he had to look for the luggage, which he had no doubt sold. The street, in the heart of old Montmartre, has hardly changed.
