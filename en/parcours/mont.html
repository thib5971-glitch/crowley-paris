    Rue Campagne-Première8 bis, rue Campagne-Première, 75014 Paris · Metro Raspail (4, 6)Private, seen from the streetLodging1902–1903

    Crowley lodged at no. 8 bis of this artists' street in January 1903 and wrote from here to Gerald Kelly, his Cambridge friend and future brother-in-law, whose studio stood almost opposite, at no. 17. It was the heart of the neighbourhood's Anglo-American circle.

    Man Ray’s studio31 bis, rue Campagne-Première, 75014 Paris · Metro Raspail (4, 6)Private, seen from the streetBackground1922–1926

    Man Ray's studio from 1922 to the 1930s. Berenice Abbott was his assistant here from 1923 to 1926. Churton recounts Crowley's affair with Abbott and lists Man Ray, at this address, among his Parisian contacts of 1928. No source says he posed here.

  4. 7
    Hôtel de Blois50, rue Vavin, 75006 Paris · Metro Vavin (4)Fate unknownLodging1908–1924

    His home base for sixteen years. He moved in on 28 April 1908, as his relationship with Rose was falling apart, returned in 1911, stayed here during the Paris Working with Victor Neuburg, from 31 December 1913 to 12 February 1914, then in 1921 and in 1924 with Leah Hirsig. The owner, M. Bourcier, gave him credit, until their eviction on 1 May 1924.

  5. 8
    Le Dôme108, boulevard du Montparnasse, 75014 Paris · Metro Vavin (4)Still openHaunt1908

    During his magical retreat of 1 to 13 October 1908 (John St. John), he had coffee and a brioche here on the first morning and met Mary Waska here on 2 October, who then followed him to his room. The café, opened in 1898, is still serving.

  6. 10
    Le Chat Blanc13, rue d'Odessa, 75014 Paris · Metro Edgar Quinet (6)GoneHaunt1902–1905

    The artists’ restaurant to which Gerald Kelly introduces him. Somerset Maugham dines here every evening and will make him the model for Oliver Haddo, the magician of his novel The Magician (1908). Arnold Bennett describes him here too. The restaurant has disappeared.

    The story →

  7. 9
    Maison Lavenue68, boulevard du Montparnasse (angle de la rue du Départ), Paris · Metro Montparnasse-Bienvenüe (4, 6, 12, 13)GoneHaunt1908

    A hotel-restaurant founded in 1854 opposite the old Gare de l'Ouest, frequented by Rodin and Falguière. Crowley stayed here in early June 1908, the day Vanity Fair published his story “Jeremiah in the Quartier Montparnasse”, and celebrated his 33rd birthday here on 12 October: kidneys, strawberry tart, Burgundy and a cigar. It closed in 1937.
