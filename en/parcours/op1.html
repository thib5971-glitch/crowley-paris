  1. 101
    Edmond Bailly's bookshop, the Librairie de l'Art indépendant11, rue de la Chaussée-d'Antin, 75009 Paris · Metro Chaussée d'Antin – La Fayette (7, 9)Other useBookshop1888-1890 (1890s)

    Edmond Bailly, a composer and publisher, ran a small bookshop and publishing house here that became the meeting place of Symbolists and occultists. Debussy, Satie, Villiers de L'Isle-Adam, Mallarmé, Péladan and Toulouse-Lautrec dropped in during the late afternoon. Churton makes it the gateway to the whole of occult Paris in the Belle Époque.

  2. 102
    Galerie Durand-Ruel – first Salon de la Rose+Croix11, rue Le Peletier, 75009 Paris · Metro Le Peletier (7) / Richelieu – Drouot (8, 9)Fate unknownSalon1892

    On 10 March 1892 the first Salon de la Rose+Croix opened here, organized by Joséphin Péladan and funded by Count Antoine de La Rochefoucauld. The crowds were so large that traffic in the neighborhood came to a standstill. Symbolist and Idealist painters exhibited, with music by Satie in the background.

  3. 103
    Chamuel's Librairie du Merveilleux and offices of L'Initiation29, rue de Trévise, 75009 Paris · Metro Cadet (7)Other useBookshop1888-1890s

    On Papus's advice, Lucien Mauchel, known as Chamuel, opened the Librairie du Merveilleux here. The address became the nerve center of Papus's occultism: the journal L'Initiation, the Martinist Order, the Independent Group for Esoteric Studies, and meetings of Jules Doinel's Gnostic Church. Papus received the curious and the initiated here every week.

  4. 104
    Théâtre de la Bodinière – the Rites of Isis18, rue Saint-Lazare, 75009 Paris · Metro Saint-Georges (12) / Trinité – d'Estienne d'Orves (12)Other useScene1899-1900

    In this small lecture and performance hall, Samuel MacGregor Mathers and his wife Moina presented their “Rites of Isis” to the public in March 1899, a theatrical reconstruction of an Egyptian cult that had begun in their temple in Auteuil. The show intrigued the press and drew curious members of fashionable Paris.

  5. 105
    Gustave Moreau's house and studio14, rue de La Rochefoucauld, 75009 Paris · Metro Trinité – d'Estienne d'Orves (12) / Saint-Georges (12)Still openResidence1895-1898

    From 1895 the painter Gustave Moreau, a guiding figure for the Symbolists, turned his house and studio into a museum of his work while living in the first-floor apartment. He visited the first Salon de la Rose+Croix without exhibiting there. Churton presents the place as a sanctuary for lovers of Symbolist art.
