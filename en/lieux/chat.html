The story

Le Chat Blanc was a small restaurant on rue d'Odessa, close to the Gare Montparnasse, where a circle of English-speaking artists and writers dined in the early 20th century. According to Somerset Maugham's recollections, the same diners met there almost every evening and gave passing visitors a rather cool welcome. Maugham met Arnold Bennett and the critic Clive Bell there. Samuel J. Rogal, author of an encyclopedia devoted to Maugham, sees it as the model for the restaurant the novelist calls “Gravier's” in Of Human Bondage (1915).

It was the painter Gerald Kelly, Crowley's future brother-in-law, who brought Maugham here, and Maugham took to dining here every evening. Crowley, back in Paris at the end of 1902 and lodging at 8 bis rue Campagne-Première in early 1903, was among the occasional visitors. Maugham describes him as a brilliant talker, boastful and amusing, whom he found dislikeable but interesting, and who held forth to him on magic and the occult. Rogal dates their meeting to 1904, but Maugham states that Crowley was then spending the winter in Paris, which points, according to Tobias Churton, to the winter of 1902-1903.

Bennett, for his part, recorded in his journal on 9 March 1905 a dinner at the Chat Blanc in Crowley's presence, noting his dirty hands, his large rings, hair that seemed to him dyed, a showy waistcoat, a fur coat and tennis shoes.

Maugham wrote The Magician in the first half of 1907; the novel appeared in 1908. In it he gave his magus, Oliver Haddo, many of Crowley's traits, while making him more sinister and attributing real powers to him. Crowley recognised himself and responded with a long review published in Vanity Fair on 30 December 1908, signed “Oliver Haddo”; Maugham claims never to have read it.

The restaurant has disappeared and the sources mention no plaque. Rue d'Odessa still exists, in the shadow of the Tour Montparnasse; Tobias Churton places the Chat Blanc at the former no. 93, now no. 13, halfway along the street on the left coming from the boulevard.
