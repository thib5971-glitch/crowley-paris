The story

Oscar Wilde, who died on 30 November 1900 at the Hôtel d'Alsace, was first buried in the Parisian cemetery of Bagneux. In 1909 his literary executor Robert Ross acquired a plot in the 89th division of Père-Lachaise and had his remains transferred there. The monument, funded by Helen Carew, was entrusted to Jacob Epstein: a large winged sphinx carved from a block of Hopton Wood stone weighing some twenty tonnes, on a base designed by Charles Holden, shown to the London press in June 1912 and then installed in Paris in September.

The figure's nudity caused outrage. Customs demanded a duty of 120 pounds, the Préfecture de la Seine had the monument covered with a tarpaulin and placed under the guard of a gendarme, and the genitals were even plastered over. The sphinx remained hidden for nearly two years; Epstein and his friends removed the tarpaulin several times, only for it to be immediately put back. To break the deadlock, Ross had a bronze plaque in the shape of a butterfly fitted, a solution the sculptor strongly disliked.

Crowley's part was long misdated: Martin Booth put it in 1912, Wikipedia in August 1914, because the Confessions tell the episode without a date. Tobias Churton finally found in Gil Blas of 5 November 1913 the notice of a "demonstration". Crowley had sent the newspapers a manifesto in the name of artistic freedom, calling the public to the monument at midday. The day before, with the help of a young American, he rigged the tarpaulin with wires so it would drop on a signal. On the day the police, under orders to let him be, stayed away: Crowley gave his speech and unveiled the sphinx in the drizzle. Three days later Epstein wrote to The Times to disown this unofficial unveiling.

The monument has continued to attract acts of devotion and vandalism: in 1961 the sphinx's genitals were broken off and have never been found; Ross's ashes were placed in the tomb in 1950. As lipstick kisses had damaged the stone, the tomb was cleaned in 2011 and surrounded by a glass barrier about two metres high.

Listed as a historic monument on 10 March 1995, the tomb can be visited freely in the 89th division. One can see Epstein's sphinx behind its glass and the inscription carved by Joseph Cribb.
