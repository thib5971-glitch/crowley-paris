The story

Settled in Paris since 1892 (MacGregor Mathers arrived on 21 May), the Matherses moved several times for lack of money. Their most lasting home, near the Bois de Boulogne, was a villa in the Auteuil district with three entrances and therefore three addresses: 87 avenue Mozart, 43 rue Ribéra and 41 rue de la Source. The Golden Dawn's Ahathoor Temple no. 7, consecrated on 6 January 1894 in the presence of Annie Horniman in the Matherses' flat at 1 avenue Duquesne, near Les Invalides, was transferred here in early 1895; Moina, who taught there, decorated its walls with figures of Egyptian gods painted in oils. Papus (Gérard Encausse) was a member, and W. B. Yeats paid the Matherses several visits in Paris between 1897 and 1899 to work with them on Celtic rituals.

Crowley, who had entered the Golden Dawn in London in 1898, was refused access to the Second Order by the London adepts. He took the night boat to Paris on 15 January 1900; after eight days there, during which he attended (perhaps again) the Rites of Isis, Mathers initiated him into the grade of Adeptus Minor at 87 avenue Mozart, under the motto Parzival. The London adepts refused to recognise this reception; a history of the order notes moreover that it does not appear in the records of the Ahathoor Temple, which further fuels the debate.

On Monday 9 April 1900 Crowley was still in London, where he made sure of Elaine Simpson's loyalty, then took the night ferry; in Paris, a long discussion with MacGregor and Moina about the revolt of the London temple made him their representative; on the 12th Mathers handed him instructions and symbols, and he left again on the 13th at 11.50 am. On 17 April he tried to retake the Second Order's premises at 36 Blythe Road in London, but the affair fizzled out and the lawsuit was lost on 28 April. The timeline places these meetings “in Paris” without giving the address; that they took place at the Auteuil villa is therefore probable but not established by the sources.

Towards the end of 1900 the Matherses left Auteuil for Montmartre. According to Tobias Churton, the old service entrance on rue Ribéra can still be seen, partly hidden by a recent building, while the one on rue de la Source has disappeared, modern buildings having replaced the villa's gardens.
