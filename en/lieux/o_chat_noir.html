The story

The Chat Noir is the most famous artistic cabaret of Montmartre. Rodolphe Salis opened it in 1881 at 84, boulevard de Rochechouart, then moved it in 1885 to a town house on rue de Laval, now rue Victor-Massé. Poems were read and songs sung there, and its shadow theater, run notably by Henri Rivière and Charles de Sivry, drew a very mixed public. The cabaret also published an illustrated paper bearing its name, which brought together the cartoonists, poets and songwriters of Montmartre.

Churton places here Erik Satie's arrival in artistic Paris: in December 1887, accompanied by the poet Contamine de Latour, the young Norman introduced himself to Salis as a “gymnopédiste” and became the cabaret's pianist. He is said to have worked on his Gymnopédies here. Péladan also frequented the establishment, although Salis had little liking for him and the cabaret's journal echoed this. In early 1893, at the height of the Boullan affair, Churton imagines the cabaret regulars joking about the “fluids” used in bewitchment, without singling out the Chat Noir in particular. The room thus served as a link between Montmartre bohemia and the more serious circles of the Chaussée-d'Antin.

The Chat Noir closed in 1897, shortly after Salis's death; other cabarets later took over the building. The building on rue Victor-Massé, a stone's throw from place Pigalle, is now private. Steinlen's cat, which has become a tourist image, mostly recalls the sign, but the 1885 address is the one Satie and the Symbolists knew. Visitors can combine this stop with nearby place Pigalle, where several cafés frequented by the same artists once stood.
