Lodging · 1927–1928

Hôtel Foyot and Hôtel Royal Condé

Gone

Address · 33, rue de Tournon et 10, rue de Condé, 75006 Paris

Metro · Odéon (4, 10)

Today · Gone. A small garden facing the Senate has replaced the hotel.

See on the map

The story

The Foyot restaurant, at 33 rue de Tournon, facing the Palais du Luxembourg, took its name from Nicolas Foyot, a former cook to Louis-Philippe, who took it over after 1848. His successor Léopold Mourier, from 1891, created dishes there that became famous, as well as the so-called Foyot sauce. The hotel above it lodged Rilke, Santayana, T. S. Eliot and Dorothy Parker. On 4 April 1894 a bomb there seriously injured the poet Laurent Tailhade; the perpetrator was never identified. The restaurant closed in 1937 and the building was demolished shortly afterwards.

Crowley lodged at the Foyot, room 21, from 26 December 1927. Gerald Yorke, a young Cambridge graduate and future financial backer, invited to meet him at the Foyot, landed at Le Bourget on 29 December; on the 31st Crowley consulted the I Ching on the significance of this meeting, and Yorke left for London on 2 January. On 5 March 1928 Kasimira Bass arrived, his new companion, whose presence soon provoked scenes with Ninette Shumway and Dorothea Walker. On 22 March the publisher Collins returned to him all rights to The Diary of a Drug Fiend. Until the end of April he divided his time between the Foyot and a house at 3 rue de Fleury in Fontainebleau, where Ninette and Lulu lived; he then returned to the Foyot, moved on 12 May to the Hôtel Royal Condé with Kasimira, and left Paris on 24 May for Cassis and Marseille.

The Hôtel Royal Condé, 10 rue de Condé, two streets away, thus housed him from 12 to 24 May 1928. The summer was spent in the South of France, then in the Alps: it was at Carry-le-Rouet, on 13 July, that Yorke joined him, guaranteed him a weekly allowance and set up a publishing syndicate. On the evening of 29 August Crowley and Kasimira returned to Paris and went back to the Royal Condé until mid-September; he then moved to 55 avenue de Suffren.

Nothing remains of the Foyot: at the corner of rue de Tournon and rue de Vaugirard there is now a small tree-planted space facing the Senate. On rue de Condé, the sources do not say what became of the Hôtel Royal Condé.
